# L01 · What Is a Computer?
**Module 1 · Stage 1 · 2 hours** · Week 1
*Deck: slides above the line, speaker notes below each slide.*

## S1 · Is this a computer?
**Notes:** [~5] TALK: hold up the calculator. No definition yet — collect votes only. ASK: "Thumbs up if it's a computer, down if not, sideways if unsure." Record the split on the board; you will resolve it at S4. MISC: many will say "no — it's just a calculator," which is exactly the tension we want. TRAN: "Let's make 'is a computer' precise enough to argue about."

## S2 · Today
- Four-part definition you can *use*
- Hardware / software / data / users
- Where computers hide (ICT)
- The course map
**Notes:** [~3] TALK: frame the hour as installing one analytical habit. ASK: none yet. TRAN: straight on.

## S3 · Try the definition on real things
- Pocket calculator: one job → special-purpose
- Wristwatch: tells time — processes *data*?
- Alarm clock: models time mechanically
**Notes:** [~7] TALK: test each prop against intuition before any formal words. ASK: "What would the watch need to do differently for you to call it a computer?" MISC: "smart = computer" — park it; the definition will separate capability from branding. DEMO: pass the calculator around during the discussion. TRAN: "Notice we keep needing four ideas: input, processing, output, storage."

## S4 · The definition
**Input → Processing → Output, with Storage — under stored, programmable instructions**
- *Programmable*: different jobs, different instructions
- *Stored*: instructions live in memory, not wiring
**Notes:** [~10] TALK: build the definition word by word; each word earns its place. Resolve the S1 vote formally: the calculator is special-purpose *by the definition's own terms*. ASK: "Which word rules the mechanical clock out — and why?" Expected: no processing of data. MISC: "processing = math" — counter with a messaging app: routing, ordering, notification are processing. TRAN: "Now run the definition on a whole scenario."

## S5 · One scenario, four layers
- Sending a message: phone (hardware), app (software), text + contacts (data), sender/receiver (users)
**Notes:** [~8] TALK: walk the scenario; fill the two-column board table from the module guide. ASK: "Do the same for ordering a ride." TRAN: "Layers scale up: the same four parts describe a data centre."

## S6 · Data becomes information
- "34.2" is data · "Your balance is 34.2" is information
**Notes:** [~7] TALK: the transformation *is* processing; this distinction returns in Module 7 as the whole point of databases. ASK: "Give me data on this slide; now give me information." MISC: "data and information are synonyms" — hold the line with the balance example. TRAN: "Where do you *meet* computers? Everywhere — mostly hidden."

## S7 · Where is the computer?
- Alarm · bus card · projector · quiz app — dozens before breakfast
- **Embedded**: a computer inside another product
**Notes:** [~7] TALK: rapid tour of the morning timeline; name embedded systems explicitly. ASK: "Name one device in this room that hides a computer." MISC: none expected here. TRAN: "Some devices make the boundary genuinely hard."

## S8 · Boundary cases
- Smart speaker? Microwave with a program dial? Mechanical clock?
**Notes:** [~6] TALK: think-pair-share, 2 minutes, then defend with the definition's own words. ASK: "Which *word* in the definition decides each case?" MISC: "if it's electronic it's a computer" — electronic alone isn't programmable processing of data. TRAN: "Let's fix that judgment call as a habit."

## S9 · The habit
**Where is the computer? What is the input? What is processed? What is the output?**
**Notes:** [~5] TALK: this four-question habit is the course's analytical tool — say that plainly. ASK: apply it to the room's projector live. TRAN: "Now the map of the next sixteen weeks."

## S10 · The course at a glance
- 8 modules · 32 lectures · labs each module · weekly quizzes
**Notes:** [~6] TALK: one guided tour, no clicks wasted. DEMO: live navigation — Schedule → Assessment → Help, projected. TROUBLE: if the network is down, use the schedule screenshot from the module guide's prep list. ASK: none. TRAN: "How you'll be graded, in one breath."

## S11 · How you'll be assessed
- Weekly quizzes (best 10 of 14) · labs · midterm · project · final
**Notes:** [~4] TALK: rhythm, not detail — the assessment page carries the specifics; weightings are flagged there as instructor-review items. ASK: "Where do you look on the site the night before a quiz?" (Schedule.) MISC: none. TRAN: "Time to use the definition yourselves."

## S12 · Activity — Find the computer (20 min)
Pairs · five devices · justify with the definition
1. List five devices in the room or your bag
2. Computer or not — decide
3. Justify each with the definition's key words
4. Hardest case to the board
**Notes:** [~20] TALK: run exactly as the lecture page's activity spec; results feed L02's opening. ASK (circulating): "Which word of the definition is doing the work in your justification?" MISC: watch for teams arguing brand ("it's just a scale") — redirect to the definition. TROUBLE: odd numbers → one trio, or join the weakest pair yourself. EXT: fast finishers classify their five devices by L03's classes (preview). TRAN: "Two minutes left — pick your hardest case."

## S13 · Quick check
1. Which definition word rules out the mechanical clock?
2. Is a bus card input, processing, output, or storage — and which part?
3. Data vs information: one sentence.
**Notes:** [~10] TALK: individual write, then pair-compare, then resolve. ASK: the three on screen. MISC: Q1 — expect "electricity" answers; steer to *processing of data under stored instructions*. TROUBLE: if Q3 fails broadly, re-run the S6 balance example before summarizing. TRAN: "Let's lock the hour down."

## S14 · Summary
- Definition: input → processing → output, stored + programmable
- Hardware / software / data / users
- Computers hide inside things; the four-question habit
**Notes:** [~6] TALK: bullets are the exam skeleton; say so. ASK: none. TRAN: one exit question.

## S15 · Exit ticket
**Where is the computer in a supermarket checkout? Name all four parts.**
**Notes:** [~6] TALK: collect slips at the door; sort them tonight into got-it / near-miss / lost — that sort is L02's opening. ASK: none. TROUBLE: no slips? Use the shortest blank paper routine — the ask stays.

# L02 · A Brief History of Computing
**Module 1 · Stage 1 · 2 hours**

## S1 · Last time: your exit tickets
**Notes:** [~5] TALK: show the checkout four-part tally from L01's sort; read one near-miss aloud *anonymized* and fix it with the room. ASK: none. TRAN: "Today: where did the four parts come from?"

## S2 · Today
- Six stages, one causal chain
- Each stage's limit → the next stage's invention
**Notes:** [~3] TALK: history as engineering Q&A, not museum tour. TRAN: begin at the beginning.

## S3 · Before electronics: automating calculation
- Mechanical calculators · programmable looms (punched cards)
**Notes:** [~8] TALK: the loom's cards are *programs* — stored instructions outside the machine. ASK: "What is the modern descendant of a punched card?" MISC: "old = irrelevant" — the loom is literally the stored-program idea's ancestor. TRAN: "Electricity changes what a switch can be."

## S4 · The electronic generations
- Vacuum tubes → transistors → integrated circuits → microprocessors
**Notes:** [~12] TALK: one portrait image per stage; per stage name the *limit* that killed it (heat/reliability → size/hand-assembly → cost → integration). ASK: "Why did tubes fail? What did the transistor fix?" MISC: "each generation was just faster" — insist on mechanism change, not speed. TRAN: "What happens when a whole CPU fits on one chip?"

## S5 · The stored program
- Instructions held in memory like data — the pivot
**Notes:** [~8] TALK: this is the lecture's conceptual centre; preview that L05 draws the machine it enables. ASK: "Why does 'instructions are data' make software *swappable*?" MISC: students understate it as "just convenient" — press the rewiring alternative. TRAN: "From room to pocket."

## S6 · Generations at a glance
- Gen table: mechanism → enabling change → new machine class
**Notes:** [~5] TALK: consolidation pass — students fill the third column from memory before revealing. ASK: per row, "what became possible?" TRAN: "The chain reaches your pocket."

## S7 · From room to pocket
- Mainframes → minis → micros (PCs) → mobile → embedded everywhere
**Notes:** [~7] TALK: emphasize *who could use* the machine at each step — institutions → departments → everyone. ASK: "Which step most changed *who* could use a computer?" MISC: none. TRAN: "Why these exact stages? The chain, rehearsed."

## S8 · Causal links, stated
- "Tubes were fragile → transistors" · "Transistors shrank → ICs" · "ICs cheapened → microprocessor"
**Notes:** [~5] TALK: model the "X enabled Y because…" sentence form — it is the jigsaw's grading currency. ASK: "Complete the next link yourself." TRAN: "Your turn to assemble the chain."

## S9 · The chain in one line
**calculation → programmable → electronic → transistor → integrated → micro → personal/mobile**
**Notes:** [~3] TALK: chant-level consolidation; it returns as the exit ticket. TRAN: activity.

## S10 · Activity — Timeline jigsaw (A1, 30 min)
Teams: order the cards · justify the causal links
**Notes:** [~30] TALK: run per the A1 handout; teams must *speak* the causal sentences, not just order cards. ASK (circulating): "Which link is your weakest — defend it." MISC: date-memorizing teams — redirect to justification; dates are not the assessed skill. TROUBLE: a lost card is fine — the argument is the point. EXT: fast teams add a predicted *next* stage with its enabling technology. TRAN: "Cards down — let's check the record."

## S11 · Quick check
1. Order: transistor, vacuum tube, IC, microprocessor.
2. Why do punched cards count as programming?
3. One "X enabled Y because…" sentence of your own.
**Notes:** [~12] TALK: write-pair-resolve; Q1 is retrieval, Q2 the concept, Q3 the skill. MISC: Q2 — expect "because they're old computers" answers; steer to *instructions stored outside the machine's mechanism*. TRAN: "The chain, in summary."

## S12 · Summary
- Six stages, each enabled by the previous stage's limit
- Stored program: the pivot that made software possible
**Notes:** [~6] TALK: restate the causal-chain thesis; the dates are disposable, the links are not. TRAN: exit ticket.

## S13 · Exit ticket
**Name two adjacent stages and write the "enabled because" link between them.**
**Notes:** [~6] TALK: sort tonight; weak links open L03. TROUBLE: slips short on justification — that *is* the signal; note it.
