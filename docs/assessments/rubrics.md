# Rubrics — How Marked Work Is Judged

Every marked component uses published criteria. The specialized rubrics — [integrated project](project/rubric.md), [problem-solving cases](../case-bank/index.md#9-assessment-rubric-all-levels), and the per-lab [60/25/15 consolidated rubric](../labs/workbook.md) — live on their own pages. This pack holds the **transversal rubrics**: the criteria reused across labs, exams, cases, and the project, so you can practise against them everywhere.

> Model answers, marking schemes, and annotated samples are instructor-only and are never published on this site — that separation is enforced by the site's build checks.

---

## How to read these rubrics

- **Weights** show where the marks are; **"full marks"** describes the distinguishable top band, not a minimum.
- Rubrics assess *reasoning quality* — a correct answer without its method or rationale earns partial credit at best (the same rule the exam's method marks use).
- The same rubric applies at every difficulty level; the *bar* rises with the task's complexity.

## Lab performance rubric

Applies to all eight graded labs (details and per-lab weights in the [lab workbook](../labs/workbook.md)).

| Criterion | Weight | Full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| Tasks completed correctly | 60% | Every step performed with evidence (screenshots/outputs where asked); methods shown, not just results |
| Working artifact | 25% | The artifact (file, sheet, diagram, configured setting) does what the lab asks and would work on someone else's machine too |
| Reflection | 15% | Honest observation *and* explanation: what you expected, what happened, why the difference — including failed attempts and what they taught |

## Written explanation rubric

Used for lab reflections, homework write-ups, and the report components.

| Criterion | Weight | Full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| Claims are supported | 40% | Each claim points at evidence from your own work (an output, a measurement, a screenshot) or a course source |
| Correct terminology | 25% | Course terms used precisely (e.g., "swapping" not "it uses the disk") — the [glossary](../glossary.md) is the standard |
| Structure | 20% | Paragraph per idea; a reader who missed the class can follow it |
| Honesty of calibration | 15% | Uncertainty stated; "I don't know why yet" beats a confident wrong mechanism |

## Troubleshooting rubric

Used in labs with diagnostic content and the exam's scenario items. The assessed skill is the *isolation method*: change one variable, classify the fault's scope.

| Criterion | Weight | Full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| Ordered isolation steps | 40% | Steps from least-invasive/most-likely first; each step states what its result would prove |
| Scope classification | 25% | The fault's layer is named (hardware/OS/application/network/data) with the evidence that places it there |
| Safety and reversibility | 20% | No destructive steps before evidence justifies them; data protected before any repair attempt |
| Verification | 15% | The fix is confirmed by a test, not declared |

## Problem analysis rubric

Used in case-bank assessments and the exam scenario. Mirrors the [case rubric's](../case-bank/index.md#9-assessment-rubric-all-levels) core criteria at component scale.

| Criterion | Weight | Full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| Problem definition | 30% | The *real* problem, not the surface symptom; stakeholders named including the non-obvious |
| Constraint awareness | 20% | All binding constraints named; implicit ones surfaced |
| Decomposition | 30% | The problem broken into tractable parts; reasoning steps explicit and ordered; assumptions labelled as assumptions |
| Trade-offs named | 20% | What the chosen path costs and who pays; risks named with mitigations |

## Solution justification rubric

Used wherever you must *defend* a choice (labs' design decisions, case answers, project report).

| Criterion | Weight | Full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit criteria | 35% | The decision's criteria are stated (weighted where the task allows) before the option is chosen |
| Comparison | 25% | Real alternatives compared — not a strawman rejected in one line |
| Counter-argument engaged | 25% | The strongest objection is stated *and* answered, not ignored |
| Claim calibration | 15% | The conclusion claims exactly what the evidence supports — open-verdict tasks stay open |

## Teamwork rubric

Applies to the paired [project](project/rubric.md) (inside its presentation/report criteria) and to graded group work where the instructor assigns it.

| Criterion | Weight | Full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution | 40% | Each member's contribution is real, visible, and attributed (per-section ownership; version history, not surveillance) |
| Collaboration | 25% | Decisions made together and recorded; disagreements resolved by criteria, not volume |
| Reliability | 25% | Agreed deadlines met; slips communicated early with a recovery plan |
| Equal participation | 10% | Both/all voices present in the deliverable and the showcase/demo |

## Exam scenario rubric

The long scenario (Section C) in the [final blueprint](exams/index.md#final-blueprint) — the same weights used by the midterm's mini-scenario, scaled to its marks.

| Criterion | Weight | Full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis | 30% | Problems identified with the evidence that discriminates them; layers classified correctly |
| Ordered response | 30% | Actions in a defensible order (protect data → diagnose → repair → verify) with the *rationale* for the order |
| Prevention/policy | 25% | The recurrence prevented structurally (habits, settings, backups), not "be more careful" |
| Communication | 15% | Course frameworks used by name; claims calibrated; a reader could act on the answer |
