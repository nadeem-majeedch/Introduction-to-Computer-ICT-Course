# Assessment Framework — How You Are Assessed

> **Important — weightings are proposals.** Every percentage below is a **proposed weighting awaiting instructor approval** (tracked as open question OQ-13). Do not rely on any number here until your instructor confirms the assessment plan in Week 1. The graded structure this site currently describes is the 15/20/20/25/20 configuration in the table; the instructor may change it, and will tell you if so.

This page is the student-facing map of the whole assessment system: what you will be assessed on, when, how each piece is marked, and the rules of academic integrity. Companion pages: the [practice question bank](question-bank/index.md), [exam blueprints](exams/index.md), [rubrics](rubrics.md), and the [project pack](project/brief.md).

## 1. The assessment plan (proposed)

| Component | Proposed weight | What it is | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quizzes — best 10 of 14 | **15%** *(proposed)* | 5–10 minute in-lecture quizzes on the two most recent lectures; practice versions with self-checks are published weekly | Weeks 1–15 |
| Practical labs | **20%** *(proposed)* | Eight graded lab tasks (one per module) plus four ungraded skill builders — see [lab workbook](../labs/workbook.md) | One per module |
| Midterm examination | **20%** *(proposed)* | 90-minute in-class exam covering Modules 1–4 (L01–L16) — see the [blueprint](exams/index.md#midterm-blueprint) | Week 9 |
| Integrated ICT project | **25%** *(proposed)* | Semester-long project in pairs: proposal → design doc → working demo → showcase + report — see the [project brief](project/brief.md) | Assigned L08; showcase L32 |
| Final examination | **20%** *(proposed)* | Comprehensive exam with emphasis on Modules 5–8 — see the [blueprint](exams/index.md#final-blueprint) | Exam period |

**Formative assessment is not graded** (proposed). Exit tickets, in-class activities A1–A5, case-bank discussions, lab reflections, and the weekly homework tasks exist to give *you* feedback — they prepare you for the graded components but earn no marks. Your instructor may introduce a participation component; if so, the whole plan changes and you will be told explicitly.

**Sum check:** 15 + 20 + 20 + 25 + 20 = 100%.

## 2. The assessment types, and what each is for

| Type | Where you meet it | Its purpose | Marked how |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Formative assessment** (ungraded) | Exit tickets, activities, drafts | Feedback for you before anything counts | Feedback only |
| **Quizzes** | Weekly, in lecture | Keep pace with lectures; reward steady understanding | Best 10 of 14 (proposed) |
| **Practical lab assessment** | Labs 1–8 | Demonstrate skills hands-on; marked 60/25/15 (tasks / artifact / reflection) — [lab rubric](rubrics.md#lab-performance-rubric) | Per-lab criteria |
| **Individual assignments** (feeds) | Weekly homework tasks | Prepare quiz/exam readiness | Not graded directly (proposed) |
| **Group activities** | Activities A1–A5, case discussions, project pairs | Practise collaboration and discussing reasoning | Feedback + project teamwork rubric |
| **Midterm examination** | Week 9 | Confirm Stages 1–2 skills before the deeper modules | See [blueprint](exams/index.md#midterm-blueprint) |
| **Final examination** | Exam period | Cumulative understanding, emphasis on later modules | See [blueprint](exams/index.md#final-blueprint) |
| **Integrated ICT project** | All semester | Synthesize four modules into one working artefact | [Project rubric](project/rubric.md) |
| **Problem-solving case assessment** | Case bank levels 1–4 in class | Train and evidence the problem-solving toolkit | [Case rubric](../case-bank/index.md#9-assessment-rubric-all-levels) |

## 3. Academic integrity — what counts, what doesn't

These rules are course-level and consistent with university policy; your instructor may add specifics.

**You may** (and are encouraged to): discuss concepts and approaches with classmates; use AI tools where the task allows, with disclosure; use the published practice quizzes, question bank, and case bank as practice; cite any source you use, including AI tools.

**You may not**: submit work that is not yours (or your pair's, for the project); copy quiz/exam answers from another student; use AI tools or phones during graded in-class quizzes and exams; share exam content between sections or semesters; use AI to generate answers for graded lab reflections without disclosure.

**AI use, in one line:** for ungraded practice and understanding, AI is fine; for anything graded, follow the task's stated rule and disclose what you used and what you changed. The [responsible-AI lecture (L28)](../lectures/L28-responsible-ai-literacy.md) gives you the verification workflow the course expects.

**Consequences** follow university procedure; the course's part is honesty and proportionality — technical incidents (e.g., a wrong LMS answer key) are corrected and communicated, not treated as student failures.

## 4. What the exam and quiz materials look like

- **Practice quizzes** (Q01–Q14): published weekly with expandable self-checks — [quizzes index](quizzes/index.md). They differ from the graded versions, whose questions and keys are never published.
- **Question bank**: ~45 practice questions across ten types with model answers and difficulty tags — [question bank](question-bank/index.md).
- **Exam blueprints**: the exact structure of the midterm and final (sections, marks, timing, difficulty mix) are published in advance — see [exam blueprints](exams/index.md). The actual items are not.
- **Rubrics**: every marked component's criteria are published — see [rubrics](rubrics.md). Model answers and marking schemes are not; that separation is enforced by the site's build checks.
