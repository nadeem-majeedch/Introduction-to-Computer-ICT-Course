# A1 — History Timeline Jigsaw

> **Lecture:** L02 · **Time:** 25 min · **Groups:** 4–5 students

## Goal

Assemble the major milestones of computing into a dated timeline — then argue the *causal* links between them. Dates matter less than reasons; the exam asks "why," not "when."

## Setup

Each group receives an envelope of cards (or the list below to copy onto cards): **device/idea cards** and **date cards** (shuffled).

Device/idea cards: abacus · Jacquard's punched-card loom · Babbage's Analytical Engine · Lovelace's notes · ENIAC · stored-program concept (EDVAC report) · transistor · integrated circuit · IBM System/360 · Intel 4004 microprocessor · Apple II / IBM PC · smartphone era.

## Round 1 — Assemble (10 min)

Arrange cards in chronological order with approximate dates. Check: the class-wide reference timeline is revealed after Round 2.

## Round 2 — Causal chains (10 min)

For five transitions, write one sentence of *cause*: "X enabled Y because …". Required chains:

1. punched cards → later computing storage/input
2. vacuum tubes → transistors → integrated circuits (two links)
3. integrated circuits → microprocessor
4. microprocessor → personal computing
5. miniaturization trend → smartphone in your pocket

## Round 3 — Defend (5 min)

Each group presents its hardest causal link; the class challenges. The instructor's notes settle disputes afterwards (not published — reasoning in [L02](../lectures/L02-a-brief-history-of-computing.md)).

## Solo variant (revision)

Write the timeline from memory, then the five causal sentences. Anything you can't link causally is your revision list.
