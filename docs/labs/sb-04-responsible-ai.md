# SB-4 — Responsible AI Use Trial

> **Related lecture:** L27–L28 · **Duration:** 30–40 min · **Practice (completion-marked)**

## Learning outcomes

1. **Evaluate** one AI tool against the L28 pre-use checklist (provenance, data use, privacy). (CLO-10)
2. **Verify** one AI-produced claim against an authoritative course source before accepting it. (CLO-10)
3. **Draft** a disclosure block for AI-assisted work that meets the project rubric's standard. (CLO-10)

## Prerequisites and equipment

L27–L28 lectures; access to *any* AI tool the course's integrity rules permit (the university's sanctioned tool, a free tier, or — if you use none — the course's provided sample outputs). **No installs.** Privacy rule: submit **no personal data** to any tool; use the sample task below, not your own documents.

## Setup

Choose your tool (or take the provided sample-output pack from the LMS). Record before use: tool name, provider, what its data-use settings say about your inputs, and whether an education/privacy mode exists.

## Step-by-step tasks

1. **Pre-use audit (10 min).** Run L28's checklist on your tool: what trained it (per its own documentation)? where does your input go? who sees the output? Three lines, cited to the tool's own settings/docs.
2. **The sample task (10 min).** Ask the tool (or read from the sample pack) a course-relevant question — e.g., "Explain two's complement in four sentences." Then **verify every claim** against L13–L14's lecture pages: mark each sentence verified / corrected / unverifiable.
3. **The disclosure block (10 min).** Write the four-part disclosure for this trial: tool, role it played, what you verified, what you changed. Compare with the project rubric's disclosure standard; adjust until they match.
4. **The boundary note (5 min).** One sentence: what task from this course would you *not* delegate to this tool, and which lecture's reasoning says so?

## Expected observations

Verification usually finds one *plausible-but-wrong* detail per four sentences — the unassigned-truth-value problem, met firsthand. The tool's data-use answer is often harder to find than expected; "not findable" is a recordable finding.

## Questions

1. Which of your tool's claims was verifiable *only* against the course pages, and why did general sources not settle it?
2. Why does the disclosure block strengthen rather than weaken the work, in the rubric's own terms?

## Troubleshooting

- *No tool access at all* → the sample-output pack carries the same tasks; the audit uses the pack's documented tool description. Every outcome stays reachable without an account.
- *Tool refuses the course question* → pick a different course topic; refusals themselves are worth a sentence (guardrail behaviour, L28).
- *Verification finds nothing wrong* → re-check your verification: "nothing wrong found" must be earned by named sources per claim, not asserted.

## Submission and assessment

Completion-marked practice: submit the audit lines, the verified/corrected sentence marking, the disclosure block, and the boundary note. Feedback against the "excellent" descriptors; not weighted into the final grade. The *graded* version of this skill is the project's disclosure requirement — this builder is its rehearsal.
