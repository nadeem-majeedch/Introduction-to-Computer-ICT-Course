# References and Recommended Resources

This list contains the textbooks and online resources the course draws on. Items marked **Primary** are the course's conceptual backbone; others are optional depth or practice. The instructor confirms editions and availability in Week 1 (open question OQ-04).

---

## Primary textbooks

1. Brookshear, J. G., & Brylow, D. *Computer Science: An Overview* (13th ed.). Pearson. — Used across Modules 1–8 as the conceptual spine; schedule references "B&B" chapters.
2. Sinha, P. K., & Sinha, P. *Computer Fundamentals*. BPB Publications. — Accessible regional availability; aligned with ICT course structures.
3. Bourgeois, D. T. *Information Systems for Business and Beyond* (open textbook, CC BY-NC). — Free online; strong for Modules 6–7 (networks, IS, and data).

## Supplementary reading

4. White, R., & Downs, T. *How Computers Work* (10th ed.). Que Publishing. — Visual, beginner-friendly hardware intuition (Module 2).
5. Patt, Y. N., & Patel, S. J. *Introduction to Computing Systems* (3rd ed.). McGraw-Hill. — Deeper treatment of bits, logic, and instruction execution (Modules 4–5; instructor reference).
6. Patterson, D. A., & Hennessy, J. L. *Computer Organization and Design*. Morgan Kaufmann. — Instructor reference for CPU/memory depth.

## How to cite what you use

- When a lecture or handout states a technical or historical fact, its *References* section names the source in author–date format (see `docs-meta/04-documentation-standards.md`, §F, in the course repository).
- For your own submissions, cite any source beyond the course materials: author, year, title, publisher/URL.

## Accessibility of resources

Videos listed above carry captions or transcripts (verify per item when assigning). If any assigned resource is inaccessible to you, tell the instructor — an accessible substitute will be provided.
