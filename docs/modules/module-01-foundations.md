# Module 1 — Foundations: The Digital World

> **Lectures:** L01–L04 · **Weeks:** 1–2 · **Stage:** 1 (Describe & identify)

## What this module is about

Before we open up a computer or write a formula, we need a shared vocabulary: what exactly *is* a computer, where did it come from, what forms does it take today, and how is it reshaping society and your career. These four lectures build that foundation — everything else in the course stands on them.

## Module learning outcomes

By the end of Module 1 you can:

- Give a precise, example-rich definition of a computer and distinguish hardware, software, data, and users. (CLO-1)
- Place major computing milestones (mechanical, electronic, transistor, integrated circuit, microprocessor, mobile) in chronological order and explain why each mattered. (CLO-1)
- Classify computers into supercomputers, mainframes, servers, personal computers, mobile devices, and embedded systems, matching each to real applications. (CLO-1)
- Discuss the digital divide, automation's effect on work, and ICT career paths with concrete evidence. (CLO-1, CLO-10)

## Study advice

Focus on *categories and reasoning*, not memorized dates. Exam questions in this module ask you to classify and explain (e.g., "which class of computer serves thousands of bank customers simultaneously, and why?"), not to recite timelines.
