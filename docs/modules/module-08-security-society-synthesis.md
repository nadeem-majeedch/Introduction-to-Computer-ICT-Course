# Module 8 — Security, Society, and Synthesis

> **Lectures:** L29–L32 · **Weeks:** 15–16 · **Stage:** 4 (Evaluate & design)

## What this module is about

The final module turns from how computers work to how we live with them: defending systems and accounts, protecting privacy, designing solutions with computational thinking, and surveying emerging technologies with open eyes. It closes with the project showcase — the semester's integrated demonstration of everything you have built.

## Lectures

| Lecture | Title | You will learn to |
|---|---|---|
| [L29](../lectures/L29-cybersecurity-fundamentals.md) | Cybersecurity Fundamentals | Recognize malware, phishing, and social engineering; apply layered defences |
| [L30](../lectures/L30-privacy-digital-citizenship.md) | Privacy and Digital Citizenship | Manage your digital footprint and apply privacy-protective habits |
| [L31](../lectures/L31-computational-thinking.md) | Computational Thinking and Problem Solving | Apply decomposition, patterns, abstraction, and algorithm design |

## Module learning outcomes

- Identify common attacks and select appropriate defences for described scenarios. (CLO-10)
- Apply password/MFA hygiene and backup discipline to your own digital life. (CLO-10)
- Design an algorithm using computational-thinking steps and express it as a flowchart. (CLO-9)
- Assess claims about IoT, blockchain, and quantum computing critically. (CLO-1, CLO-10)
- Present the integrated project and reflect on the course's connected picture. (CLO-11)

## Study advice

The final exam emphasizes Modules 5–8, so this module carries double weight: content in its own right, plus the scenario-response question that asks you to combine security, privacy, and AI-literacy judgement in one realistic situation.
