# Module 4 — Data and Digital Representation

> **Lectures:** L13–L16 · **Weeks:** 7–8 · **Stage:** 2 (Represent & manipulate)

## What this module is about

Everything a computer has ever done — music, films, messages, spacecraft telemetry — is patterns of 0s and 1s. Module 4 teaches you to read those patterns: number systems and conversions, how signed integers really work, how characters become bytes, and how images, sound, and video are captured and compressed.

## Module learning outcomes

- Perform conversions among binary, octal, decimal, and hexadecimal, showing method. (CLO-4)
- Represent negative integers in two's complement and predict overflow behaviour. (CLO-4)
- Explain how ASCII, Unicode, and UTF-8 relate, and diagnose common encoding failures (mojibake). (CLO-4)
- Describe how raster images, audio, and video are digitized, and choose compression approaches for given needs. (CLO-4)

## Study advice

Conversion is a skill, not knowledge: practise until you can convert 8-bit binary ↔ decimal ↔ hex in under a minute each. The two's complement "flip and add one" trick makes sense only after you understand why it works — L14 builds that understanding.
