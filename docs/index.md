# Introduction to Computer (ICT)

Welcome! This is the official course website for **Introduction to Computer (ICT)** — a first-semester course for BS Computer Science and BS Data Science students.

## What this course is

A complete introduction to how computers work and how to work with them. Over **32 lectures (2 hours each, 64 contact hours)** you will move from everyday computer use to a solid conceptual model of computing — hardware, software, data, networks, cloud, databases, intelligent systems, and the security and ethics that come with them. No prior computing background is assumed.

## What you will be able to do

By the end of the semester you will be able to:

- Explain how a computer executes a program, from the CPU to memory and storage.
- Convert between number systems and explain how text, images, audio, and video are represented.
- Read truth tables and simple logic circuits — the foundation of all digital hardware.
- Use documents, spreadsheets, and presentations professionally.
- Trace what happens when you open a web page: networks, protocols, the cloud.
- Design a small database, prepare data for analysis, and evaluate AI claims critically.
- Protect your accounts, data, and privacy — and behave as a responsible digital citizen.

The full list of measurable outcomes is in the [Syllabus](syllabus.md).

| [8. Security, Society & Synthesis](modules/module-08-security-society-synthesis.md) | 15–16 | Cybersecurity, privacy, computational thinking, emerging tech, project showcase |

## Study expectations

Plan for **2–3 hours of self-study per week** beyond class time: review the lecture page, attempt the *Check your understanding* questions, and complete the lab on time. Concepts in this course build on each other — falling behind early makes later weeks much harder, so ask for help early (see [Help](help.md)).
